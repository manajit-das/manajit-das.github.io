About Me

I am currently pursuing my PhD in Computational Chemistry at IIT Bombay, where I have the privilege of working in Prof. R. B. Sunoj's lab. My research focuses on exploring the intricate mechanisms of chemical reactions using advanced computational techniques.

Beyond my academic endeavors, I am passionate about data analysis, teaching, and engaging in interdisciplinary research collaborations. I work at the intersection of quantum chemistry, machine learning, and reaction mechanism elucidation.

My recent work involves applying machine learning and chemical language models to predict molecular properties and reaction outcomes — bridging the gap between traditional computational chemistry and modern AI methods.

Research Interests

01

Computational Reaction Mechanisms

Elucidating the mechanistic pathways of organic and organometallic reactions using density functional theory (DFT) and ab initio methods. Particular interest in solvent effects and transition state modeling.

DFT Transition States Solvent Effects
02

Machine Learning in Chemistry

Developing and applying ML models — including chemical language models and graph neural networks — for predicting molecular properties, reaction outcomes, and enantioselectivity in asymmetric catalysis.

ML Language Models Selectivity Prediction
03

Asymmetric Catalysis

Understanding the origin of stereoselectivity in transition-metal and organocatalytic reactions through combined computational and data-driven approaches, including Heck and relay catalytic cycles.

Enantioselectivity Organocatalysis Heck Reaction
04

Data-Driven Molecular Science

Integrating high-throughput computational screening with statistical analysis and visualization tools to extract chemical insights from large molecular datasets and reaction databases.

Data Analysis Cheminformatics High-Throughput

Molecular Insights on Solvent Effects in Organic Reactions as Obtained through Computational Chemistry Tools

Das, M.; Gogoi, A.; Sunoj, R. B.

Journal of Organic Chemistry · 2022, 87, 1630–1640

Abstract: Solvents play a crucial yet often underappreciated role in determining the outcome of organic reactions. This perspective examines how modern computational chemistry tools — including implicit and explicit solvation models, microsolvation approaches, and molecular dynamics — can reveal the molecular-level origins of solvent effects on reaction rates, selectivities, and mechanisms. Selected case studies illustrate how these computational insights complement experimental observations and guide synthetic design.

2022

Machine Learning Assisted Enantioselectivity Prediction of Asymmetric Relay Heck Reaction

Das, M.; Sharma, P.; Sunoj, R. B.

Journal of Chemical Physics · 2022, 156, 114303

Abstract: Predicting enantioselectivity remains a central challenge in asymmetric catalysis. This work develops machine learning models trained on DFT-computed features to predict the enantiomeric excess of an asymmetric relay Heck reaction. The study demonstrates that descriptor-based ML models can accurately capture the subtle steric and electronic factors governing facial selectivity, offering a computationally efficient alternative to exhaustive transition-state searches for catalyst screening.

2024

Advances in Machine Learning with Chemical Language Models in Molecular Property and Reaction Outcome Predictions

Das, M.; Ghosh, A.; Sunoj, R. B.

Journal of Computational Chemistry · 2024, 45, 1160–1176

Abstract: Chemical language models (CLMs) — large pre-trained models operating on SMILES, SELFIES, and related molecular string representations — have emerged as powerful tools for molecular property prediction and reaction outcome forecasting. This review surveys recent advances in CLMs, examines their architectures, training strategies, and benchmark performance, and discusses applications ranging from physicochemical property prediction to retrosynthesis and reaction yield forecasting. Perspectives on current limitations and future directions in the field are also provided.
